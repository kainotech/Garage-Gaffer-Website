# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Non-technical UK drivers in Bristol and the surrounding area, roughly 30-50 years old. They own a car that needs work done — servicing, a repair, an MOT, diagnostics — and are tired of the usual hassle of ringing round garages, leaving voicemails, and getting vague verbal quotes. They're not car experts and don't want to become one; they want someone trustworthy to just sort it.

## Product Purpose

Garage Gaffer is a Bristol car-repair booking platform. A visitor enters their number plate (or car details) and postcode, gets an instant fixed price, picks a slot, and books — no bidding, no waiting for callbacks. In this MVP phase, the booked work is carried out at a trusted partner garage (the customer drops the car off), with mobile mechanics-who-come-to-you planned as a later phase. Success is a visitor trusting the site enough to submit their reg and postcode and complete a booking on the first visit.

## Brand Personality

Honest, warm, straightforward. Confident but never clinical or corporate. Plain English, no jargon, slightly informal British tone (e.g. "no fuss", "done right first time"). The brand should feel like a trustworthy local business, not a tech startup.

## Anti-references

Generic SaaS landing page aesthetics. Icon-in-a-colored-box feature grids. Floating decorative badges. Gradient glows and glassmorphism. Big glossy 3D icons. All-caps eyebrow labels stacked above every heading. Numbered step markers used decoratively rather than for genuine sequences. Anything that reads as templated or AI-generated rather than considered.

## Design Principles

- Plain-spoken confidence over salesy hype — say exactly what happens next, never oversell.
- Restraint over decoration — one deliberate bold moment per page, not scattered effects on every section.
- Trust through specificity — real facts (vetting, warranty terms, fixed pricing) do the persuading, not vague reassurance copy.
- Visual consistency across pages — reuse the same type system and green accent language rather than inventing a new treatment per section.
- Minimal cognitive load — a non-technical visitor should always know what to do next without thinking hard about it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA expectations: readable contrast, visible keyboard focus states, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected everywhere (already established practice in this codebase). No additional stated requirements beyond that.
